### **Phase 1: Model (Cognitive Apprenticeship)**

> * **Theoretical Grounding:** Cognitive apprenticeship emphasizes making internal, expert thinking explicit. In traditional trade apprenticeships, physical skills are modeled; in cognitive apprenticeship, the instructor models the **mental processes and math logic** behind trade calculations.  
> * **Instructional Strategy:**  
  * **"Think-Aloud" Demonstrations:** When calculating heat transfer (*Q*\=*U*⋅*A*⋅Δ*T*), perform the calculation step-by-step on a board or digital display while narrating *why* each step is taken:*"I need to calculate the heat loss through this 100 sq ft wall section. First, I'm identifying the R-value of the insulation... then converting R-value to U-factor using decimals (U\=R1​)... now I multiply by the area and temperature difference."*  
  * **Contextual Framing:** Explicitly link decimal operations and unit conversions directly to energy efficiency outcomes (e.g., lower heat loss \= lower energy consumption \= lower carbon footprint).

### **Phase 2: ZoPD (Zone of Proximal Development & Scaffolding)**

> * **Theoretical Grounding:** Vygotsky defined ZPD as the distance between what a learner can do independently and what they can achieve with guidance from a More Knowledgeable Other (MKO)—either the instructor or a peer.  
> * **Instructional Strategy:**  
  * **Heterogeneous Grouping:** Pair participants with complementary math comfort levels. A student stronger in decimals can support a peer focused on trade application, reinforcing learning for both.  
  * **Targeted Instructor Prompts:** As you circulate the room, avoid giving immediate answers. Instead, offer tiered scaffolding prompts:  
    * *Level 1 (Conceptual):* "What are we trying to measure in this geothermal loop ratio?"  
    * *Level 2 (Procedural):* "Which operation do we use when converting inches to decimal feet?"  
    * *Level 3 (Direct):* "Take a look at your decimal placement in step 2."  
  * **Real-World Green Scenarios:**  
    * *Solar Panel Output:* Calculating array square footage, decimal efficiency rates, and daily kilowatt-hour generation.  
    * *Insulation & Retrofits:* Calculating material board-feet, decimal waste factors, and thermal resistance increases for building envelopes.  
    * *HVAC & Geothermal:* Sizing ductwork surface areas, heat exchange rates, and airflow volumes using decimal/fractional conversions.

### **Phase 3: Fade & Assess (Self-Reliance & Portfolio Artifacts)**

> * **Theoretical Grounding:** As students build competence, instructor scaffolding gradually "fades," transferring full responsibility and ownership of the problem-solving process to the learner.  
> * **Instructional Strategy:**  
  * **Individual Assessments:** Traditional written and performance-based tests to ensure each participant meets NABTU / MC3 proficiency standards in Unit 5\.  
  * **Digital Portfolios (Padlet / Miro):**  
    * **Student-Led Presentations:** Have participants upload solved green math scenarios, blueprint markups, or retrofit estimates to Miro boards or Padlet walls.  
    * **Peer Feedback & Pride:** Participants explain their calculations in short audio/video clips or text notes on the board, demonstrating mastery.  
    * **Partner Visibility:** These digital boards serve as clean, visual artifacts that NEW can highlight to partners like BlackRock and NYPA as proof of 21st-century workforce readiness and innovative curriculum design.

### **Core Pedagogical & Policy Enhancements (Extracted from *AE Spring 2024*)**

#### **1\. Authentic Context over Applied Context (Stone’s NRCCTE Principle)**

> * **The Insight:** James R. Stone III emphasizes that CTE curricula must begin with **authentic technical requirements**, not abstract math with a thin coat of trade context.  
> * **Application to your 48-Hour Course:** Rather than presenting standard decimal worksheets and calling them "green," each lesson begins directly with the trade problem (e.g., *Sizing ductwork for an all-electric VRF heat pump system in a high-rise retrofit*). The math (fractions, decimals, ratios) is taught as the essential technical tool required to solve the trade problem.

#### **2\. Cognitive Apprenticeship & Psychological Safety (Kirschner et al. / Rosenshine)**

> * **The Insight:** Rosenshine's principles and Cognitive Apprenticeship require explicit modeling by experts, followed by guided practice in a **psychologically safe environment** where novices feel secure asking questions.  
> * **Application to your 48-Hour Course:**  
  * **Instructor Modeling:** Step-by-step "Think-Alouds" for complex green calculations (e.g., heat loss, board feet, solar shading).  
  * **Peer-to-Peer Scaffolding (ZoPD):** Mixed-ability group work where participants collaborate on green retrofit proposals, lowering math anxiety and accelerating skill mastery.  
  * **Fading to Digital Artifacts:** Transitioning to student-led Padlet/Miro board presentations, allowing participants to demonstrate their math logic visually to funders and apprenticeship coordinators.

#### **3\. Climate Justice & Just Transition (Vachon / Karp Framework)**

> * **The Insight:** Todd E. Vachon highlights the importance of preparing workers for a **"Just Transition"**—connecting industrial labor, green technology, environmental advocacy, and high-wage union careers.  
> * **Application to your 48-Hour Course:** Frame the green math curriculum as empowering women at NEW to become leaders in New York’s green economy—capable of both building clean energy infrastructure and calculating its direct community and environmental benefits.
